# Target screening

**Screen:** TSX-listed copper-gold producers with a market capitalisation of roughly
C$1–10Bn, producing today (not developers), with at least two operating or
construction-stage assets. Market data comes from `scripts/fetch_market_data.py`
(snapshot in `data/market/`).

## Why Centerra acquires Ero

1. **Balance sheet room.** Centerra carries essentially no debt and roughly US$450M of
   cash, with a funded growth plan at Mount Milligan. It can pay part of the price in cash
   and still keep leverage below 1.0x EBITDA. Ero is the reverse: copper growth is built,
   but its senior notes and the tail of its growth capex absorb cash flow.
2. **Complementary metal mix.** Centerra is roughly 70% gold by revenue and its copper is
   partly streamed to Royal Gold. Ero is roughly 85% copper. The combined company is a
   balanced Americas-weighted copper-gold producer, which the market has historically
   paid a premium for over single-metal mid-tiers.
3. **Mine life and jurisdiction.** Centerra's Öksüt mine runs down by 2029 and its growth
   (Goldfield, the Mount Milligan expansion) is gold. Ero adds two long-life Brazilian
   copper operations (Caraíba and the newly built Tucumã), diversifying away from
   Türkiye-heavy free cash flow.
4. **Overlapping skill sets.** Both run large-scale open-pit and underground copper
   concentrators and sell concentrate to the same smelter market, which supports
   procurement and marketing synergies.

## Why not the alternatives

- **Capstone** would be a merger of equals at best, and its value depends on a Chilean
  ramp-up that Centerra has no edge in.
- **Torex / OceanaGold** add more gold, not copper, and duplicate Centerra's exposure.
- **SSR Mining** adds Türkiye risk on top of Öksüt and has almost no copper.
